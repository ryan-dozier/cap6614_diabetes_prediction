## 2. Target definition

Synthetic preparation retains supplied binary `diagnosed_diabetes` labels and validates values in {0,1}; it does not reconstruct a clinical diagnosis. Equivalence of the synthetic label-generation process to NHANES is not established.

NHANES [DIQ_I / DIQ010](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2015/DataFiles/DIQ_I.htm) asks whether a doctor or health professional has ever told the participant they have diabetes, excluding pregnancy-related diagnosis in the applicable wording. Code maps yes (1) to 1 and no (2) to 0. Borderline (3), refused, don't-know and missing responses remain unmapped and are excluded from complete-case cohorts.

This is reported diagnosed diabetes, not laboratory-defined diabetes. A negative response does not establish absence of undiagnosed disease. Matching target names do not establish identical endpoints.

The downloader lists 18 components; preparation loads 14. Linked codebooks below document the 2015–2016 variable definitions, component eligibility and skip patterns. Project inclusion additionally requires age >=20 and feature-set-specific complete cases. Component eligibility alone does not guarantee usable values.

`GHB_I`, `GLU_I`, `INS_I`, `OGTT_I` are downloaded but not loaded by this preparation. They do not define the target, enter predictors or determine cohort eligibility in this implementation. Not every column in a loaded component is used.

Unmapped responses generally remain missing, not negative. Structural skips become zero only where explicitly coded. Alcohol caveat: the ordinary calculation accepts ALQ130 only from 1 to 14, omitting valid top-code 15 (15 drinks or more). This can contribute to missingness; impact on otherwise eligible participants was not counted. No mapping was changed in this audit.

## 4. Cohorts, exclusions and fixed splits

Preparation validates nonmissing unique SEQN, starts with DEMO adults aged >=20, and left-joins other loaded components one-to-one. It then requires every selected predictor and the mapped target, without learned imputation.

| Saved cohort | Predictors | Participants | Positive labels |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Adult harmonized base | Candidate table | 5,719 | Not supplied as an aggregate count |
| strict_common_16 | 16 | 4,306 | 676 |
| common_20 | 20 | 3,908 | 606 |
| common_22 | 22 | 1,717 | 275 |

Counts are from Newton `results/nhanes_preparation/metadata.json`, supplied during this audit, not a newly executed run. Exclusion causes can overlap; these totals do not uniquely attribute losses to each variable.

Feature-set hierarchy:

- Synthetic baseline has 24 shared predictors.
- common_22 excludes diet_score and waist_to_hip_ratio: defensible equivalents were not established in selected components.
- common_20 additionally excludes LDL and triglycerides for coverage.
- strict_common_16 additionally excludes education, employment, income and screen time because their mappings are more approximate.

Adding predictors changes both available information and participant eligibility. The common_22 reduction is not solely attributable to fasting; every other complete-case requirement still applies. Prepared sensitivity cohorts do not imply completed sensitivity experiments.

The primary cohort is stratified by target into nominal 70/15/15 splits using seeds 42 and 43, with integer rounding:

| Split | Rows | Positive labels |
|---|---:|---:|
| Train | 3,014 | 473 |
| Validation | 646 | 102 |
| Test | 646 | 101 |

Preparation saves `data/splits/nhanes_split_assignments.csv` (row_index, seqn, split). The loader checks primary alignment, joins each feature-set cohort by SEQN one-to-one and rejects missing assignments. Retained participants cannot change split with feature set; sensitivity subsets need not retain exact proportions. SEQN, synthetic row indices and competition IDs are provenance, not predictors. Compare common participants where necessary to separate added-feature effects from cohort effects.

Synthetic preparation retains supplied labels and the 24 frozen predictors, excluding competition id and six Original Synthetic-only variables: diabetes_risk_score, diabetes_stage, glucose_fasting, glucose_postprandial, hba1c, insulin_level. It validates missingness and reports duplicates without automatically removing them. Disjoint row indices alone do not prove absence of repeated feature vectors or generator dependence.

## 5. Survey design and cross-domain interpretation

Preparation saves WTMEC2YR, WTSAF2YR, SDMVPSU and SDMVSTRA separately in `data/processed/nhanes_weights.csv`, not as predictors. At the audited revision, `src/experiments/metrics.py:evaluate_binary` computes metrics, prevalence and calibration summaries without sample weights or survey-design adjustments. This statement concerns that function, not every diagnostic script. Its results describe the selected evaluation cohort, not survey-weighted national prevalence or nationally representative predictive performance.

Population inference requires component-appropriate weights and variance estimation; examination and fasting-subsample weights are not interchangeable. Complete-case selection introduces further limitations not automatically resolved by released weights. See component analytic notes and [NHANES guidelines](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/nchs/nhanes/analyticguidelines.aspx). Participant bootstrap uncertainty is not automatically complex-survey uncertainty.

Interpret comparisons with these limits:

- Original Synthetic versus Playground is synthetic-to-synthetic shift; their stated generator-training relationship does not establish independent real-world cohorts.
- Shared feature names do not prove identical measurement methods, time windows or generation semantics. Reported history, examination measurements and derived questionnaire quantities are different evidence types.
- Reported diagnosis and synthetic labels may represent different outcomes. Adult-only, complete-case and fasting-related selection limit generalization.
- Top-coding and category collapsing lose information; approximate mappings remain approximate even after category names match.
- Feature-set comparisons can change both predictors and participants. Ranking, calibration and threshold metrics also answer different questions; single-seed results do not establish superiority.
- BRFSS is not an evaluation domain in this predictive design. Downloaded biomarkers and available feature sets must not be described as completed experiments.

## 6. Evidence limitations and follow-up

Not captured: exact historical downloads; historical Original Synthetic version label; explicit competition file-release version; local NHANES component hashes/revision identities; full synthetic generator/label provenance; uniquely attributable exclusion counts; impact of individual recoding concerns. These are evidence limitations, not invented dates or assurances of equivalence. Amend this record if materially incompatible source evidence is discovered.

Separate follow-up work may evaluate alcohol top-code handling, approximate category mappings, blood-pressure averaging or survey-weighted analysis. Such work requires its own review and versioned evidence; it must not silently overwrite existing inputs, splits or artifacts. D07 documents these concerns without claiming they have been corrected.
